Motor Circuit Design and Installation: Device Selection, Cable Size, Starting Voltage Drop, Control Panels, Testing and a Troubleshooting Table

This page brings every previous chapter together into one design job, from the load to commissioning, with what to check on site and a troubleshooting table compiled from the most common symptoms. Every number in the examples can be calculated from the formulas on the page.

By Ajarn ModUpdated
In 30 seconds
  • Design in order: load → motor → starting method → devices → cable → panel → test
  • The cable must pass three checks: current rating, voltage drop (running ≤ 3–5 %, starting ≤ 10–15 %) and earth-fault current at the cable end
  • Panel heat: \(\Delta T = P/(kA)\) — a drive almost always means a fan is needed
  • Before energising: megger ≥ 1 MΩ, check tightness, check phase sequence, test the control circuit on its own

The 10-step design procedure Basic

K1 Shaft power of a pump or a drive

\[ P_{pump} = \frac{\rho g Q H}{\eta_p} \qquad P = T\,\omega = \frac{T\,n}{9550}\ \text{(kW)} \]

K2 Motor size margin

\[ P_{motor} \ge (1.10\text{–}1.15)\,P_{load,max} \;\rightarrow\; \text{next standard size} \]

Cable size, voltage drop and fault current at the cable end Intermediate

K3 Cable ampacity after derating

\[ I_z\,k_{temp}\,k_{group} \ge I_n \qquad I_z \ge I_r\ (\text{overload protection setting}) \]

K4 Running voltage drop

\[ \Delta V\% = \frac{\sqrt3\,I_n\,L\,(R\cos\varphi + X\sin\varphi)}{V_L}\times100 \le 3\text{–}5\,\% \qquad R = \frac{\rho}{A},\ \rho_{Cu,70^\circ C} \approx 0.0225\ \Omega\,\text{mm}^2/\text{m} \]

K5 Starting voltage drop

\[ \Delta V_{st}\% \approx \Delta V_{tr}\% + \frac{\sqrt3\,I_{st}\,L\,(R\cdot0.3 + X\cdot0.95)}{V_L}\times100 \le 10\text{–}15\,\% \]

K6 Earth fault at the cable end (TN system)

\[ I_{f} = \frac{U_0}{Z_s} \ge I_a \qquad Z_s \approx Z_{src} + (1 + m)\,\frac{\rho L}{A_{ph}}\ \ (m = A_{ph}/A_{PE}) \]

\(U_0\) = 230 V · \(I_a\) = the current that makes the protective device disconnect within the time required by the standard (for fixed motor circuits in a TN system usually 5 s; use the magnetic trip value of the MPCB/breaker for instantaneous disconnection). Take Ik and disconnection times from the installation standard that applies in the country concerned.

Example K-1

A 15 kW 4-pole 400 V pump 50 m from the panel, 315 kVA transformer with uk 4 %, direct-on-line start

  1. M6: \(I_n = 15{,}000/(\sqrt3 \times 400 \times 0.921 \times 0.84) = 28.0\) A · \(I_{st} = 7 \times 28 = 196\) A

  2. L2 (at the transformer): \(S_{sc} = 315/0.04 = 7.9\) MVA · \(S_{st} = \sqrt3(400)(196) = 136\) kVA → \(\Delta U \approx 1.7\,\%\)

  3. K3: 6 mm² cable \(I_z \approx 36\) A × 0.87 = 31.3 A ≥ 28 A ✓

  4. K4: \(R = 0.0225/6 = 3.75\) mΩ/m → \(\Delta V = \sqrt3(28)(50)(0.00375 \times 0.84 + 0.00008 \times 0.54) = 7.7\) V = 1.9 % ✓

  5. K5: \(\sqrt3(196)(50)(0.00375 \times 0.3 + 0.00008 \times 0.95) = 20.4\) V = 5.1 % + 1.7 % = 6.8 % ✓ → direct-on-line starting is acceptable

  6. Devices: 32 A AC-3 contactor · 24–32 A MPCB set to 28 A · 6 mm² PE

Control panels: layout, IP rating and heat Intermediate

  • Low-voltage assemblies are designed and verified to IEC 61439 (the panel builder must verify temperature-rise limits, clearances and the busbar short-circuit rating)
  • IP54 for general factory areas, IP55/65 for wash-down or outdoor areas (IEC 60529) — a cut-out for a fan must use a fan-and-filter unit with the same IP rating as the enclosure
  • Leave 20–30 % spare space · keep the clearances in the drive manual (100–200 mm above and below for airflow)

K7 Enclosure temperature rise and fan airflow

\[ \Delta T = \frac{P_{loss}}{k\,A_e}\ \ (k \approx 5.5\ \text{W/m}^2\text{K painted steel}) \qquad \dot V\,[\text{m}^3/\text{h}] \approx \frac{3.1\,P_{loss}\,[\text{W}]}{\Delta T_{allow}} \]
Example K-2

A wall-mounted 600 × 800 × 300 mm enclosure containing a 15 kW drive (losses ~2.5 %)

  1. \(P_{loss} \approx 0.025 \times 15{,}000 + 40 = 415\) W (including contactors and the power supply)

  2. Effective cooling surface (not counting the back against the wall) \(A_e \approx 0.6(0.8) + 0.6(0.3) + 2(0.8)(0.3) = 1.14\) m²

  3. K7: sealed \(\Delta T = 415/(5.5 \times 1.14) = 66\) K — in a 35 °C plant room the inside would exceed 100 °C ✗

  4. Use a fan: allow 10 K above ambient → \(\dot V = 3.1(415)/10 = 129\) m³/h → choose a fan-filter unit ≥ 160 m³/h (allowing for a clogged filter)

Pre-commissioning tests Basic

K8 Insulation resistance acceptance

\[ R_{iso} \ge 1\ \text{M}\Omega\ @\ 500\ \text{V DC (IEC 60204-1)} \qquad R_{40^\circ C} \ge 5\ \text{M}\Omega\ \text{windings} < 1\ \text{kV (IEEE 43)} \qquad PI = \frac{R_{10\,min}}{R_{1\,min}} \ge 2 \]
  1. Visual inspection: devices match the drawings, cable sizes, wire numbers, all covers fitted, no wire offcuts left in the panel
  2. Tightness: every power terminal torqued to the specified value (paint-mark after tightening)
  3. Earth continuity: measure the PE resistance from the motor frame to the panel earth bar
  4. Insulation resistance: disconnect drives, soft starters and electronics first, then test the cable and motor at 500 V DC (K8)
  5. Control circuit: isolate the power circuit (switch the MPCBs off), supply control power only, and test the sequence, interlocks, timers and emergency stop
  6. Direction of rotation: uncouple if possible, bump-start briefly and check the direction (a pump run backwards for long can be damaged)
  7. Live run: measure three-phase currents, voltage, temperature and vibration, compare with the nameplate and record them as the baseline

Troubleshooting table

SymptomPossible causesWhat to check
Press START and the contactor does not pull inBlown control fuse · overload still tripped · STOP/E-stop latched · loose wireMeasure voltage point by point from the L rail along the drawing
Pulls in while pressed but drops out on releaseSelf-holding contact (13-14) not wired or faultyMeasure across 13-14 while energised, check the wire in parallel with the START button
Contactor hums loudlyLow coil voltage · dirty pole faces · broken shading ringMeasure coil voltage while energised, clean the pole faces
Motor hums but does not turnLost phase · jammed load · wrongly connected in Y for a heavily loaded 400/690 V motorMeasure three-phase voltage at the motor terminals, turn the shaft by hand (isolate first)
Overload trips after a few minutes' runningGenuine overload · wrong setting · low or unbalanced voltage · failed bearingMeasure current against the nameplate, measure voltage, listen to the bearings
MPCB trips instantly at startShort circuit · failed insulation · magnetic setting too low · star–delta changeover timing wrongMegger, measure winding resistance, note when in the sequence it trips
Motor abnormally hot but does not tripBlocked fan/fins · low-speed running on a VFD · harmonics · high ambient temperatureMeasure frame temperature, clean, check the operating frequency
Contactor contacts burn out quicklyUndersized · frequent inching (AC-4) · coil not pulling in firmlyCompare the rating with the duty, check operations per hour

Preventive maintenance

  • Monthly: listen, check temperatures, clean panel fan filters, check pilot lamps
  • Every 6 months: infrared thermal scan under load (to find loose connections), measure three-phase currents
  • Yearly: re-torque power terminals, inspect contactor contacts, measure insulation resistance and record the trend, test emergency stops
  • By running hours: grease bearings as stated on the motor plate, replace the drive cooling fan (often every 3–5 years) and the DC-bus capacitors per the manual
