Why history belongs in an engineering education

I believe what separates a good engineer from a technician is knowing where a formula comes from and how far it can be trusted. History is the shortest route to that understanding, because every theory began with a real problem someone was stuck on. Fourier was stuck on heat conduction, Heaviside on telegraph lines, Shewhart on telephone equipment failing underground. Once you know the original problem, you see what question a formula answers, and you stop using it in the wrong place.

History also tells you something textbooks rarely say: much knowledge was discovered more than once in different countries, some was wrong for decades before anyone corrected it, and the person whose name is on a formula was not always the first. Knowing this makes you a more critical reader of any textbook.

The order in which the disciplines appeared

Military engineering came first: forts, ramparts and siege engines were the first work to carry the name. Imhotep, who designed Pharaoh Djoser's step pyramid in Egypt around 2650 BC, is often called the first engineer whose name history records. After that each discipline split off as society needed it.

  • Civil engineering separated from military engineering in the mid-eighteenth century. The École des Ponts et Chaussées opened in 1747 and Britain's Institution of Civil Engineers in 1818.
  • Mechanical engineering came from the steam engine and the Industrial Revolution. The Institution of Mechanical Engineers was founded in 1847.
  • Electrical engineering came from the telegraph and electric lighting. The world's first chair of electrical engineering was created at Darmstadt in 1882.
  • Chemical engineering arose when the chemical industry had to scale up. MIT opened its programme in 1888.
  • Industrial engineering grew out of scientific management. The first programme opened at Penn State in 1908.

Mathematics underlies all of them, so it has its own section, arranged in the order engineering students learn it.

Engineering in Thailand

University engineering education in Thailand began at Chulalongkorn University, which had a Faculty of Engineering from its founding in 1917, continuing an earlier engineering school within the Civil Service School. The Engineering Institute of Thailand was founded in 1943, and the Council of Engineers was established under the Engineer Act of 1999 to regulate licensed engineering practice. Each section includes episodes from Thailand, such as the first electric light in the Grand Palace, the first railway, Bhumibol Dam and natural gas from the Gulf of Thailand.
