History of industrial engineering: engineers who design systems, people and processes

Other engineers design machines, bridges or circuits. Industrial engineers design how people, machines, materials and information work together. That makes it the youngest of the five major disciplines and the one most concerned with people.

Overview: when the system matters as much as the machine

In The Wealth of Nations (1776) Adam Smith described a pin factory that split the work into eighteen operations and produced hundreds of times more than a craftsman doing every step alone. That observation is the central question of industrial engineering: how to organise work for the best result. Yet nobody answered it systematically, by measurement and experiment, until the late nineteenth century.

The first industrial engineering programme opened at Penn State in 1908 under Hugo Diemer. The field then grew along four lines: work study from Taylor and the Gilbreths, operations research from the Second World War, statistical quality control from Bell Labs, and lean production from Toyota. All four come together in every industrial engineering curriculum today.

Industrial engineering and Thailand

Thai industry absorbed this discipline largely from Japan, because Japanese car and electronics makers set up production in Thailand from the 1960s. Toyota Motor Thailand was founded in 1962, and words such as kaizen, 5S, kanban and TPM became everyday language on Thai factory floors. The Thai Industrial Standards Institute was set up in 1968, and ISO 9000 spread through Thai factories from the 1990s.
