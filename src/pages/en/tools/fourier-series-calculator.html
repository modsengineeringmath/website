How to use it

  1. Pick a ready-made waveform, or type \(f(t)\) piece by piece. For example piece 1: 1 from 0 to pi; piece 2: -1 from pi to 2*pi.
  2. Enter the period \(T\), equal to the total length of the pieces. pi is accepted, e.g. 2*pi.
  3. Choose the number of harmonics \(N\) and calculate. Then drag the slider under the graph to see the partial sum approach \(f(t)\).

The coefficient formulas

A periodic function \(f(t)\) with period \(T\) and fundamental angular frequency \(\omega_0 = \dfrac{2\pi}{T}\) has the Fourier series

\[ f(t) = a_0 + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(a_n\cos n\omega_0 t + b_n\sin n\omega_0 t\right) \] \[ a_0 = \frac{1}{T}\int_{T} f(t)\,dt, \qquad a_n = \frac{2}{T}\int_{T} f(t)\cos n\omega_0 t\,dt, \qquad b_n = \frac{2}{T}\int_{T} f(t)\sin n\omega_0 t\,dt \]

Here \(a_0\) is the mean value itself. Some textbooks write \(\dfrac{a_0}{2}\) instead, which makes their \(a_0\) twice the value shown on this page.

Symmetry saves work

SymmetryConditionConsequence
Even\(f(-t) = f(t)\)\(b_n = 0\): cosine terms only
Odd\(f(-t) = -f(t)\)\(a_0 = a_n = 0\): sine terms only
Half-wave\(f(t + T/2) = -f(t)\)odd harmonics only

The calculator detects symmetry from the computed coefficients and reports it, a quick way to check a hand calculation.

Example: the square wave

The square wave \(f(t) = 1\) for \(0 \le t < \pi\) and \(-1\) for \(\pi \le t < 2\pi\) is odd and has half-wave symmetry, so only \(b_n\) for odd \(n\) survives.

\[ b_n = \frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^{\pi}\sin nt\,dt = \frac{2}{n\pi}\left(1 - \cos n\pi\right) = \frac{4}{n\pi}\ (n\ \text{odd}) \] \[ f(t) = \frac{4}{\pi}\left(\sin t + \frac{1}{3}\sin 3t + \frac{1}{5}\sin 5t + \cdots\right) \]

The THD of a square wave is 48.34 %, which is what the calculator reports. The overshoot near each jump, about 9 % of the jump, is the Gibbs phenomenon; it does not disappear as harmonics are added.
