# History of mechanics and machines: from the lever to the robot

## From Newton to the engineer's mechanics

Newton gave the laws of motion in 1687 but wrote in geometry. Euler turned them into differential equations that could be computed, and extended them to spinning rigid bodies. Lagrange in 1788 rewrote all of mechanics in terms of energy and was proud that his book contained no figures at all, only analysis.

Engineers in factories needed a simpler language. Coriolis and Poncelet at the École Polytechnique in the 1820s and 1830s taught mechanics through work and energy. The term travail, work as force times distance, is Coriolis's from 1829, and it turned questions such as how many tonnes of water an engine lifts per hour into calculations.

## Axles that broke from fatigue

In May 1842 a train returning from Versailles to Paris derailed when a locomotive axle broke; carriages with locked doors caught fire and dozens died. The axle had not been overloaded; it broke after long service under alternating stress. A German railway engineer, Wöhler, studied the problem systematically in the 1850s to 1870s and found that the stress a material can bear falls with the number of cycles, down to a limit.

The lesson had to be relearned. In 1954 two de Havilland Comets, the first jet airliners, broke up within months of each other. Water-tank tests showed fatigue starting at the corners of fuselage openings, which is why aircraft windows have had rounded corners ever since. Machine design courses in the third year give fatigue as much weight as static strength for this reason.

## Mechanisms become a science

By the late nineteenth century factory machines had increasingly intricate mechanisms. Reuleaux proposed in 1875 that every mechanism is a chain of pairs of parts in relative motion, such as revolute, sliding and screw pairs. That made it possible to count a mechanism's degrees of freedom and design new ones systematically. In the twentieth century the same idea was applied to robot arms: mechanisms in which each joint is driven by an electric motor under position control.

## Key people

### Ismail al-Jazari
1136–1206 · Upper Mesopotamia
Court engineer at Diyarbakır. His book describes fifty machines with illustrations and building instructions, including a water pump that converts rotary to reciprocating motion, water clocks and musical automata. It is one of the first detailed mechanical engineering manuals.

### Joseph-Louis Lagrange
1736–1813 · Italy / France
Rewrote mechanics in terms of energy rather than force, so that problems with complicated constraints reduce to one set of equations. Lagrange's equations are still the standard way to derive the dynamics of robot arms and electromechanical systems.

### Gaspard-Gustave de Coriolis
1792–1843 · France
A professor at the École Polytechnique who proposed the word work for force times distance and put the half in front of $mv^2$ for kinetic energy, so engineers could reason about machines in terms of work and energy. The Coriolis force in rotating frames is also his.

### August Wöhler
1819–1914 · Germany
A Prussian railway engineer who built machines to load rotating axles through millions of reversals. He found that iron breaks under repeated stress far below its static strength, and that below a certain stress it never breaks. The S–N diagram used for every shaft design today comes from him.

### Franz Reuleaux
1829–1905 · Germany
Broke every mechanism into pairs of parts in relative motion, making mechanism design a science instead of an art. He built more than 800 teaching models, and the Reuleaux triangle, a shape of constant width, lives on in the Wankel rotary engine.

## Frequently asked questions

How many simple machines are there?
Hero of Alexandria listed five: lever, wheel and axle, pulley, wedge and screw. Renaissance writers added the inclined plane as a sixth.

How was metal fatigue discovered?
Through railway axles breaking under loads below their static strength, notably after the Versailles rail accident of 1842. August Wöhler then tested it systematically and found the S–N curve and the fatigue limit.

What is Lagrangian mechanics used for in engineering?
For deriving the equations of motion of systems with complicated constraints, such as robot arms, vibrating systems and electromechanical devices, starting from kinetic and potential energy instead of force diagrams.
