    12 Determinant Properties That Appear on Every Exam: det(kA), det(A⁻¹), det(adj A) and the Traps

    Most determinant exam questions do not ask you to compute from numbers. They give a single value of det⁡A\det AdetA and ask for the determinant of a matrix built from AAA. Every such question is a one-liner once you know these 12 properties. Throughout, AAA and BBB are n×nn\times nn×n and kkk is a constant.

    By Ajarn ModUpdated 25 Oct 2026
    Three formulas to know by heart
    • det⁡(kA)=kndet⁡A\det(kA) = k^n\det Adet(kA)=kndetA — the exponent is the size nnn
    • det⁡(AB)=det⁡Adet⁡B\det(AB) = \det A\det Bdet(AB)=detAdetB, hence det⁡A−1=1/det⁡A\det A^{-1} = 1/\det AdetA−1=1/detA and det⁡Am=(det⁡A)m\det A^m = (\det A)^mdetAm=(detA)m
    • det⁡(adj⁡A)=(det⁡A)n−1\det(\operatorname{adj}A) = (\det A)^{n-1}det(adjA)=(detA)n−1

    Why the properties hold: the determinant is an area factor

    A 2×2 matrix turns the unit square into a parallelogram, and ∣det⁡A∣|\det A|∣detA∣ is its area (for 3×3, a volume). Seen this way most properties are obvious: stretch every side by 2 and the area grows by 222^222, the volume by 232^323.

    Properties 1–6: row operations and special matrices

    1. Transpose: det⁡AT=det⁡A\det A^T = \det AdetAT=detA — so every row rule also works for columns
    2. Swap two rows: the sign changes
    3. Multiply one row by kkk: the determinant is multiplied by kkk (factors come out one row at a time)
    4. Add a multiple of another row Ri←Ri+kRjR_i \leftarrow R_i + kR_jRi​←Ri​+kRj​: no change — the main tool of row reduction
    5. A zero row, two equal rows or proportional rows: det⁡=0\det = 0det=0
    6. Triangular or diagonal matrix: det⁡\detdet = product of the diagonal entries
    Example

    If ∣abcdefghi∣=5\begin{vmatrix} a & b & c \\ d & e & f \\ g & h & i \end{vmatrix} = 5​adg​beh​cfi​​=5, find ∣2a2b2cghid+3ae+3bf+3c∣\begin{vmatrix} 2a & 2b & 2c \\ g & h & i \\ d + 3a & e + 3b & f + 3c \end{vmatrix}​2agd+3a​2bhe+3b​2cif+3c​​

    1. Take 2 out of row 1 (property 3) · subtract a multiple of row 1 from row 3 to get (d,e,f)(d, e, f)(d,e,f) without changing the value (property 4)

    2. The rows are now (a,b,c),(g,h,i),(d,e,f)(a, b, c), (g, h, i), (d, e, f)(a,b,c),(g,h,i),(d,e,f); swapping rows 2 and 3 flips the sign (property 2)

    2×(−5)=−102\times(-5) = -102×(−5)=−10

    Properties 7–10: constants, products, inverses and powers

    1. det⁡(kA)=kndet⁡A\det(kA) = k^n\det Adet(kA)=kndetA — because kkk sits in all nnn rows
    2. det⁡(AB)=det⁡Adet⁡B=det⁡(BA)\det(AB) = \det A\det B = \det(BA)det(AB)=detAdetB=det(BA), even though AB≠BAAB \ne BAAB=BA
    3. det⁡(A−1)=1det⁡A\det(A^{-1}) = \dfrac{1}{\det A}det(A−1)=detA1​ — and AAA is invertible exactly when det⁡A≠0\det A \ne 0detA=0
    4. det⁡(Am)=(det⁡A)m\det(A^m) = (\det A)^mdet(Am)=(detA)m

    • det⁡(A+B)≠det⁡A+det⁡B\det(A + B) \ne \det A + \det Bdet(A+B)=detA+detB — add the matrices first (only row-by-row linearity exists)
    • det⁡(kA)≠kdet⁡A\det(kA) \ne k\det Adet(kA)=kdetA and det⁡(adj⁡A)≠det⁡A\det(\operatorname{adj}A) \ne \det Adet(adjA)=detA — the exponent depends on nnn

    Frequently asked questions

    Is det(2A) equal to 2 det A?

    No. Multiplying a matrix by 2 multiplies every row by 2, and a factor 2 can be taken out of each row once. For an n×n matrix det(2A) = 2ⁿ det A, so a 3×3 matrix gives 8 det A.

    How do you find det(adj A)?

    Use det(adj A) = (det A)ⁿ⁻¹, which follows from A adj A = (det A)I: taking determinants of both sides gives det A · det(adj A) = (det A)ⁿ. For a 3×3 matrix with det A = −2, det(adj A) = 4.

    Is det(A + B) equal to det A + det B?

    No. Determinants are not additive over whole matrices; add the matrices first and then take the determinant. Linearity only holds one row at a time.

    Which row operation leaves the determinant unchanged?

    Adding a multiple of one row to another (R_i ← R_i + kR_j) leaves it unchanged. Swapping two rows changes the sign, and multiplying one row by k multiplies the determinant by k.
