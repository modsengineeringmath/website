    What is a Taylor series?

    To find the polynomial that looks most like f(x)f(x)f(x) near a point aaa, force its value, slope, curvature and every higher derivative at aaa to match those of fff. The result is the coefficients ck=f(k)(a)k!c_k = \dfrac{f^{(k)}(a)}{k!}ck​=k!f(k)(a)​ and the series

    f(x)=f(a)+f′(a)(x−a)+f′′(a)2!(x−a)2+f′′′(a)3!(x−a)3+⋯f(x) = f(a) + f'(a)(x - a) + \frac{f''(a)}{2!}(x - a)^2 + \frac{f'''(a)}{3!}(x - a)^3 + \cdotsf(x)=f(a)+f′(a)(x−a)+2!f′′(a)​(x−a)2+3!f′′′(a)​(x−a)3+⋯

    The first two terms f(a)+f′(a)(x−a)f(a) + f'(a)(x - a)f(a)+f′(a)(x−a) are the tangent line, the linearization that engineers use more than any other approximation. Each extra term makes the polynomial follow the function over a wider range.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is the difference between a Taylor series and a Maclaurin series?

    A Maclaurin series is a Taylor series expanded about a=0a = 0a=0, a special case. The formula is the same with a=0a = 0a=0 substituted.

    How many terms are enough?

    It depends on the accuracy you need and the distance ∣x−a∣|x - a|∣x−a∣. Use the Lagrange remainder bound ∣Rn∣≤M∣x−a∣n+1/(n+1)!|R_n| \le M|x - a|^{n+1}/(n+1)!∣Rn​∣≤M∣x−a∣n+1/(n+1)! and choose n so the bound is below your tolerance. The closer x is to a, the fewer terms you need.

    Does a Taylor series work for every x?

    Not always, only inside the radius of convergence R. For example ex, sin⁡x, cos⁡xe^x,\ \sin x,\ \cos xex, sinx, cosx have R=∞R = \inftyR=∞, but ln⁡(1+x)\ln(1 + x)ln(1+x) and 1/(1−x)1/(1 - x)1/(1−x) have R=1R = 1R=1. Outside that range, adding more terms never approaches the true value.

    What do engineers use Taylor series for?

    To approximate complicated functions with polynomials, for example sin⁡θ≈θ\sin\theta \approx \thetasinθ≈θ for pendulums and control systems, linearization about an operating point in small-signal models of diodes and transistors, and computing sin and eˣ on microcontrollers.
