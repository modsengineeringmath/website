    Cramer's Rule: How to Use It, Why It Works, and When Not to Use It

    Cramer's rule gives a direct formula for each unknown of a linear system — no row reduction and no inverse. It is perfect for exam questions that ask for a single unknown, but it has limits you must know. This article collects how to use it, where it comes from, the special cases and its practical range in one place.

    By Ajarn ModUpdated 25 Oct 2026
    The 30-second summary
    • xi=ΔiΔx_i = \dfrac{\Delta_i}{\Delta}xi​=ΔΔi​​, where Δ=det⁡A\Delta = \det AΔ=detA and Δi\Delta_iΔi​ is the determinant of AAA with column iii replaced by the constants b\mathbf bb
    • Valid when AAA is square and Δ≠0\Delta \ne 0Δ=0 · one unknown needs only two determinants
    • For systems larger than 3×3, or in software, use Gaussian elimination or LU instead

    The rule in one sentence

    Compute the determinant Δ\DeltaΔ of the coefficient matrix, put the right-hand constants in place of the column of the unknown you want, compute the new determinant Δi\Delta_iΔi​, and divide.

    3x+2y=7x−y=−1⇒Δ=∣321−1∣=−5,x=∣72−1−1∣−5=−5−5=1,y=∣371−1∣−5=−10−5=2\begin{aligned} 3x + 2y &= 7 \\ x - y &= -1 \end{aligned} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \Delta = \begin{vmatrix} 3 & 2 \\ 1 & -1 \end{vmatrix} = -5,\quad x = \frac{\begin{vmatrix} 7 & 2 \\ -1 & -1 \end{vmatrix}}{-5} = \frac{-5}{-5} = 1,\quad y = \frac{\begin{vmatrix} 3 & 7 \\ 1 & -1 \end{vmatrix}}{-5} = \frac{-10}{-5} = 23x+2yx−y​=7=−1​⇒Δ=​31​2−1​​=−5,x=−5​7−1​2−1​​​=−5−5​=1,y=−5​31​7−1​​​=−5−10​=2

    3×3 systems: solve only for what is asked

    Example

    Find yyy from 2x+y−z=3, x+3y+2z=14, 3x−y+z=22x + y - z = 3,\ x + 3y + 2z = 14,\ 3x - y + z = 22x+y−z=3, x+3y+2z=14, 3x−y+z=2

    1. Δ=∣21−11323−11∣=2(3+2)−1(1−6)+(−1)(−1−9)=25\Delta = \begin{vmatrix} 2 & 1 & -1 \\ 1 & 3 & 2 \\ 3 & -1 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = 2(3 + 2) - 1(1 - 6) + (-1)(-1 - 9) = 25Δ=​213​13−1​−121​​=2(3+2)−1(1−6)+(−1)(−1−9)=25
    2. Δy=∣23−11142321∣=2(14−4)−3(1−6)+(−1)(2−42)=75\Delta_y = \begin{vmatrix} 2 & 3 & -1 \\ 1 & 14 & 2 \\ 3 & 2 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = 2(14 - 4) - 3(1 - 6) + (-1)(2 - 42) = 75Δy​=​213​3142​−121​​=2(14−4)−3(1−6)+(−1)(2−42)=75

    y=75/25=3y = 75/25 = 3y=75/25=3 — no need to find xxx and zzz at all

    All three unknowns of a 3×3 system need four determinants. Always check by substituting back, or use the step-by-step Cramer calculator (in Thai).

    Why the rule works (a two-line proof)

    Let XiX_iXi​ be the identity matrix with column iii replaced by the solution vector x\mathbf xx. Multiplying by AAA, column iii becomes Ax=bA\mathbf x = \mathbf bAx=b while the other columns stay those of AAA, so AXi=AiAX_i = A_iAXi​=Ai​. Now use det⁡(AB)=det⁡Adet⁡B\det(AB) = \det A\det Bdet(AB)=detAdetB:

    det⁡A⋅det⁡Xi=det⁡Ai,det⁡Xi=xi ⇒ xi=det⁡Aidet⁡A\det A\cdot\det X_i = \det A_i,\qquad \det X_i = x_i \ \Rightarrow\ x_i = \frac{\det A_i}{\det A}detA⋅detXi​=detAi​,detXi​=xi​ ⇒ xi​=detAdetAi​​

    Seen another way, Cramer's rule is x=A−1b=adj⁡A bdet⁡A\mathbf x = A^{-1}\mathbf b = \dfrac{\operatorname{adj}A\,\mathbf b}{\det A}x=A−1b=detAadjAb​ written one unknown at a time, so it always agrees with the inverse method.

    When Δ = 0, and parameter-k problems

    If Δ=0\Delta = 0Δ=0 the formula fails, and the system has either no solution or infinitely many. A favourite exam example:

    kx+y=1x+ky=1Δ=k2−1,Δx=Δy=k−1\begin{aligned} kx + y &= 1 \\ x + ky &= 1 \end{aligned}\qquad \Delta = k^2 - 1,\quad \Delta_x = \Delta_y = k - 1kx+yx+ky​=1=1​Δ=k2−1,Δx​=Δy​=k−1
    • k≠±1k \ne \pm1k=±1: x=y=1k+1x = y = \dfrac{1}{k + 1}x=y=k+11​
    • k=1k = 1k=1: Δ=Δx=Δy=0\Delta = \Delta_x = \Delta_y = 0Δ=Δx​=Δy​=0; both equations are the same line → infinitely many solutions
    • k=−1k = -1k=−1: Δ=0\Delta = 0Δ=0 but Δx=−2≠0\Delta_x = -2 \ne 0Δx​=−2=0 → parallel lines → no solution
    Trap Never cancel the factor (k−1)(k - 1)(k−1) before checking k=1k = 1k=1. And for 3×3 systems, even if Δ\DeltaΔ and every Δi\Delta_iΔi​ are zero the system can still have no solution — use rank to decide (in Thai).

    Frequently asked questions

    When can Cramer's rule be used?

    When the number of equations equals the number of unknowns (a square coefficient matrix) and the determinant of the coefficient matrix is non-zero. If Δ = 0 the system has either no solution or infinitely many, and row reduction is needed to tell which.

    Why is Cramer's rule fast in exams?

    If the question asks for only one unknown you need just two determinants, Δ and the determinant for that unknown, instead of solving for everything or inverting the whole matrix. It is ideal for 2–3 unknowns by hand.

    Why don't computer programs use Cramer's rule?

    The cost explodes. With cofactor expansion a 20-unknown system needs about 5 × 10¹⁹ multiplications, while Gaussian elimination needs about 2,700, and Gaussian elimination with partial pivoting is also more stable numerically.

    Can Cramer's rule be used for AC circuits?

    Yes. It uses only addition, subtraction, multiplication and division, so it works directly with complex impedances, and also with the Laplace variable s, where Δ(s) = 0 is the characteristic equation of the circuit.
