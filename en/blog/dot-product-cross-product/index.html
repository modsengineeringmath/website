Dot Product vs Cross Product: Formulas, Meaning and Examples

Two vectors can be multiplied in two ways. The first gives a number; the second gives a new vector. Both have clear physical meanings: the dot product is used for work and angles, while the cross product is used for torque and area.

By Ajarn ModUpdated 16 Oct 2026
30-second summary
  • Dot: a⋅b=∣a∣∣b∣cos⁡θ=axbx+ayby+azbz\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}=|\mathbf{a}||\mathbf{b}|\cos\theta=a_xb_x+a_yb_y+a_zb_za⋅b=∣a∣∣b∣cosθ=ax​bx​+ay​by​+az​bz​ → scalar
  • Cross: ∣a×b∣=∣a∣∣b∣sin⁡θ|\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}|=|\mathbf{a}||\mathbf{b}|\sin\theta∣a×b∣=∣a∣∣b∣sinθ → vector perpendicular to both, direction by the right-hand rule
  • Dot = 0 ⇒ perpendicular · Cross = 0 ⇒ parallel

Dot product (scalar product)

For a=(ax,ay,az)\mathbf{a}=(a_x,a_y,a_z)a=(ax​,ay​,az​) and b=(bx,by,bz)\mathbf{b}=(b_x,b_y,b_z)b=(bx​,by​,bz​), multiply the components along the same axis and add them.

a⋅b=axbx+ayby+azbz=∣a∣∣b∣cos⁡θ\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}=a_xb_x+a_yb_y+a_zb_z=|\mathbf{a}||\mathbf{b}|\cos\thetaa⋅b=ax​bx​+ay​by​+az​bz​=∣a∣∣b∣cosθ

So the angle between the vectors follows from cos⁡θ=a⋅b∣a∣∣b∣\cos\theta=\dfrac{\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}}{|\mathbf{a}||\mathbf{b}|}cosθ=∣a∣∣b∣a⋅b​. A positive value means an acute angle, a negative value an obtuse angle.

How it is used in electrical engineering

  • Real power: treating the voltage and current phasors (RMS values) as vectors in a 2D plane, the real power P=VIcos⁡φP=VI\cos\varphiP=VIcosφ is their dot product.
  • Force on a conductor: F=I L×B\mathbf{F}=I\,\mathbf{L}\times\mathbf{B}F=IL×B and the Lorentz force F=q v×B\mathbf{F}=q\,\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B}F=qv×B are cross products.
  • Motor torque: τ=r×F\boldsymbol{\tau}=\mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{F}τ=r×F.

Frequently asked questions

How do the results of the dot product and cross product differ?

The dot product gives a scalar (a single number). The cross product gives a vector perpendicular to both vectors, with its direction given by the right-hand rule. The cross product is defined only for 3D vectors.

What does it mean if the dot product is zero?

The two vectors are perpendicular (when neither is the zero vector). For example, a force perpendicular to the motion does zero work.

What does it mean if the cross product is the zero vector?

The two vectors are parallel or lie along the same line, because sin⁡θ=0\sin\theta = 0sinθ=0.

Why does the sign change when you swap the order of a cross product?

Because the direction of the result is set by the right-hand rule. Swapping the order, b×a\mathbf{b}\times\mathbf{a}b×a, reverses the direction, so b×a=− a×b\mathbf{b}\times\mathbf{a}=-\,\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}b×a=−a×b. The dot product is commutative.
