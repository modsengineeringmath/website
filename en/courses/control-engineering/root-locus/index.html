    Root Locus: Principles and Hand Sketching

    The root locus is the path of the closed-loop poles as the gain goes from zero to infinity. The angle and magnitude conditions, six rules for sketching by hand, and a worked third-order servo example including the choice of gain for a required damping ratio.

    Taught by Ajarn ModUpdated 12 Nov 2026About 45 min readฉบับภาษาไทย →

    What you can do after this unit

    • Explain what the root locus is and which design question it answers
    • Test with the angle condition whether a point is on the locus, and find the gain there with the magnitude condition
    • Sketch a root locus by hand using the rules for real-axis segments, asymptotes, breakaway points and imaginary-axis crossings
    • Choose the gain from the root locus for a specified damping ratio

    The picture to keep in mind: turn the knob and watch the poles walk

    Suppose the controller has one gain knob. As the knob turns, the closed-loop poles move in the sss-plane. Trace their path from zero gain to infinite gain and you have the root locus.

    Unit 6 showed that pole positions set the overshoot and settling time. Unit 8 showed when the poles cross into the right half-plane. The root locus puts both in one picture: you see at once which gain gives which response, and if no gain gives what is wanted, you also see which way the path must be bent.

    The shape of the root locus depends only on the poles and zeros of the open loop, not on KKK. KKK only says how far along the path the poles have travelled.

    Choosing the gain from the root locus

    Example 9.2 The gain for ζ = 0.5

    For the system of Example 9.1 find KKK so that the dominant poles have ζ=0.5\zeta = 0.5ζ=0.5.

    The result exposes a limit: at ζ=0.5\zeta = 0.5ζ=0.5 the system takes almost 10 seconds to settle. Raising the gain to speed it up walks the poles towards the imaginary axis and increases the ringing. This locus does not pass through the region that is both fast and well damped, so the shape of the locus must be changed with a compensator, the subject of the next unit.

    Compare with tuning a drive

    Raise the speed-loop gain of a servo drive step by step and the machine first responds faster, then begins to vibrate and finally howls. That sequence is a walk along the root locus from the breakaway point to the imaginary axis. The frequency of the howl is ω\omegaω at the crossing.

    Summary of Unit 9

    • The root locus is the path of the closed-loop poles as KKK goes from 0 to ∞\infty∞.
    • A point is on the locus when ∠L(s)=±180°(2k+1)\angle L(s) = \pm 180°(2k + 1)∠L(s)=±180°(2k+1), and the gain there is 1/∣L(s)∣1/\lvert L(s)\rvert1/∣L(s)∣.
    • Branches start at open-loop poles and end at zeros or at infinity along asymptotes.
    • An added pole pushes the locus to the right; an added zero pulls it to the left.
