    The Laplace Transform

    The definition of the Laplace transform, existence and the region of convergence, basic pairs proved from the definition, every key property, the initial and final value theorems and solving differential equations, with a lab and exercises.

    Taught by Ajarn ModUpdated 20 Nov 2026About 90 min readฉบับภาษาไทย →

    What you can do after this unit

    • Write the definition of the Laplace transform and state the existence condition and region of convergence (ROC)
    • Prove the basic pairs (step, exponential, sine, cosine, ramp) from the definition
    • Use the properties (linearity, differentiation, integration, s-shift, t-shift, convolution) to transform more complex functions
    • Apply the initial and final value theorems and check when they are valid
    • Solve linear differential equations with initial conditions by Laplace, and link the result to the transfer function

    Why transform

    The differential equation of a plant can be solved directly, but every time you must guess a solution, fit constants and handle the initial conditions.

    The Laplace transform makes differentiation into multiplication by sss. A differential equation becomes an algebraic one, and the initial conditions enter the equation from the start.

    More important, it gives the transfer function, the common language of this course, and it ties time to the sss-plane we built in Unit 2.

    Definition

    The (one-sided) Laplace transform of f(t)f(t)f(t) for t≥0t \ge 0t≥0 is

    F(s)=L{f(t)}=∫0−∞f(t) e−st dt,s=σ+jωF(s) = \mathcal{L}\{f(t)\} = \int_{0^-}^{\infty} f(t)\,e^{-st}\,dt, \qquad s = \sigma + j\omegaF(s)=L{f(t)}=∫0−∞​f(t)e−stdt,s=σ+jω

    We use the lower limit 0−0^-0− so that an impulse at t=0t = 0t=0 and the initial conditions before a switch closes are included.

    Since e−st=e−σte−jωte^{-st} = e^{-\sigma t}e^{-j\omega t}e−st=e−σte−jωt, the factor e−σte^{-\sigma t}e−σt tames functions that grow exponentially, so the integral can converge if σ\sigmaσ is large enough.

    Existence and the region of convergence (ROC)

    If there are constants MMM and α\alphaα such that ∣f(t)∣≤Meαt\lvert f(t)\rvert \le M e^{\alpha t}∣f(t)∣≤Meαt for t≥0t \ge 0t≥0 (called exponential order), the integral converges for

    Re⁡ s>α\operatorname{Re}\,s > \alphaRes>α

    This is the region of convergence (ROC), a half-plane to the right of a vertical line.

    The function et2e^{t^2}et2 grows faster than any exponential, so it has no Laplace transform.

    ROC and poles

    For a signal that starts at t=0t=0t=0, the ROC is always to the right of the rightmost pole of F(s)F(s)F(s). In control work we usually quote F(s)F(s)F(s) without the ROC because we know it is a right-sided one.

    From Laplace to the transfer function

    If all initial conditions are zero, Example 3.3 gives

    Summary

    • The Laplace transform turns derivatives into multiplication by sss and differential equations into algebra
    • For a signal starting at t=0t = 0t=0 the ROC is to the right of the rightmost pole
    • The main properties are linearity, differentiation, shift in sss, shift in ttt and convolution
    • The final value theorem holds only when the poles of sF(s)sF(s)sF(s) are strictly in the left half-plane
    • The transfer function is the ratio Y/UY/UY/U with zero initial conditions
