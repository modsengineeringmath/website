    Complex Variables, Complex Functions, Mapping and the s-Plane

    The complex-variable foundation for control engineering: analytic functions, the Cauchy-Riemann conditions, conformal mapping, the s-plane, poles and zeros and the argument principle, with an interactive mapping lab and exercises.

    Taught by Ajarn ModUpdated 18 Nov 2026About 90 min readฉบับภาษาไทย →

    What you can do after this unit

    • Compute with complex numbers in rectangular and polar form, and evaluate F(s) at a complex point
    • Decide whether a complex function is analytic using the Cauchy-Riemann conditions
    • Explain the conformal property and find the image of a line under simple functions
    • Read pole locations on the s-plane to predict the response and find ζ and ωn
    • Recognize that the frequency response F(jω) is the image of the imaginary axis, and see where the argument principle comes from

    Why control engineering needs complex numbers

    Every linear time-invariant system has solutions of the form este^{st}est, where sss is a constant.

    Substitute y=esty = e^{st}y=est into y˙+3y=0\dot y + 3y = 0y˙​+3y=0.

    sest+3est=0  ⟹  s=−3s e^{st} + 3 e^{st} = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; s = -3sest+3est=0⟹s=−3

    Here sss is real. But the second-order equation y¨+2y˙+5y=0\ddot y + 2\dot y + 5y = 0y¨​+2y˙​+5y=0 gives

    s2+2s+5=0  ⟹  s=−1±j2s^2 + 2s + 5 = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; s = -1 \pm j2s2+2s+5=0⟹s=−1±j2

    Now sss is complex, and e(−1+j2)t=e−t(cos⁡2t+jsin⁡2t)e^{(-1+j2)t} = e^{-t}(\cos 2t + j\sin 2t)e(−1+j2)t=e−t(cos2t+jsin2t) is an oscillation that dies away.

    So a single number sss carries both the decay rate (its real part) and the oscillation frequency (its imaginary part). This unit builds the language we use for the rest of the course.

    Complex numbers

    Write s=σ+jωs = \sigma + j\omegas=σ+jω, with σ=Re⁡ s\sigma = \operatorname{Re}\,sσ=Res and ω=Im⁡ s\omega = \operatorname{Im}\,sω=Ims. Engineers use jjj rather than iii so it does not clash with current.

    In polar form we use magnitude and angle.

    s=∣s∣ ejθ,∣s∣=σ2+ω2,θ=arg⁡s=atan2⁡(ω,σ)s = \lvert s\rvert\, e^{j\theta}, \qquad \lvert s \rvert = \sqrt{\sigma^2 + \omega^2}, \qquad \theta = \arg s = \operatorname{atan2}(\omega,\sigma)s=∣s∣ejθ,∣s∣=σ2+ω2​,θ=args=atan2(ω,σ)

    Euler's formula links the two forms.

    ejθ=cos⁡θ+jsin⁡θe^{j\theta} = \cos\theta + j\sin\thetaejθ=cosθ+jsinθ

    The conjugate of sss is sˉ=σ−jω\bar s = \sigma - j\omegasˉ=σ−jω, and ssˉ=∣s∣2s\bar s = \lvert s\rvert^2ssˉ=∣s∣2.

    Common operations

    • Multiply. Magnitudes multiply and angles add: ∣s1s2∣=∣s1∣∣s2∣, arg⁡(s1s2)=arg⁡s1+arg⁡s2\lvert s_1 s_2\rvert = \lvert s_1\rvert\lvert s_2\rvert,\ \arg(s_1 s_2) = \arg s_1 + \arg s_2∣s1​s2​∣=∣s1​∣∣s2​∣, arg(s1​s2​)=args1​+args2​
    • Divide. Magnitudes divide and angles subtract
    • Divide in rectangular form. Multiply numerator and denominator by the conjugate of the denominator

    Analytic functions and the Cauchy-Riemann conditions

    The complex derivative is defined just like the real one.

    F′(s0)=lim⁡Δs→0F(s0+Δs)−F(s0)ΔsF'(s_0) = \lim_{\Delta s \to 0}\frac{F(s_0 + \Delta s) - F(s_0)}{\Delta s}F′(s0​)=Δs→0lim​ΔsF(s0​+Δs)−F(s0​)​

    The key difference is that Δs\Delta sΔs is complex, so it can approach zero from any direction in the plane, and the limit must be the same for every direction.

    A function that has a derivative throughout a region is called an analytic function.

    Approach along the horizontal, Δs=Δσ\Delta s = \Delta\sigmaΔs=Δσ, and along the vertical, Δs=jΔω\Delta s = j\Delta\omegaΔs=jΔω, and force the two results to agree. This gives

    ∂u∂σ=∂v∂ω,∂u∂ω=−∂v∂σ\frac{\partial u}{\partial \sigma} = \frac{\partial v}{\partial \omega}, \qquad \frac{\partial u}{\partial \omega} = -\frac{\partial v}{\partial \sigma}∂σ∂u​=∂ω∂v​,∂ω∂u​=−∂σ∂v​

    These are the Cauchy-Riemann conditions. For an analytic function the derivative is

    F′(s)=∂u∂σ+j ∂v∂σF'(s) = \frac{\partial u}{\partial \sigma} + j\,\frac{\partial v}{\partial \sigma}F′(s)=∂σ∂u​+j∂σ∂v​

    Conformal mapping

    If FFF is analytic at s0s_0s0​, a small step around s0s_0s0​ gives

    F(s0+Δs)≈F(s0)+F′(s0) ΔsF(s_0 + \Delta s) \approx F(s_0) + F'(s_0)\,\Delta sF(s0​+Δs)≈F(s0​)+F′(s0​)Δs

    Because F′(s0)F'(s_0)F′(s0​) is one complex number for every direction, we can write F′(s0)=∣F′∣ejφF'(s_0) = \lvert F'\rvert e^{j\varphi}F′(s0​)=∣F′∣ejφ.

    The result is that every small displacement is rotated by the same angle φ\varphiφ and stretched by the same factor ∣F′∣\lvert F' \rvert∣F′∣.

    The conformal property

    If F′(s0)≠0F'(s_0) \ne 0F′(s0​)=0, the map w=F(s)w = F(s)w=F(s) preserves angles (both size and sense) at s0s_0s0​. The image of an orthogonal grid is still orthogonal.

    Lengths are scaled by ∣F′∣\lvert F'\rvert∣F′∣ and areas by ∣F′∣2\lvert F'\rvert^2∣F′∣2.

    At a critical point, where F′(s0)=0F'(s_0) = 0F′(s0​)=0, the property fails. If F(s)−F(s0)F(s) - F(s_0)F(s)−F(s0​) has a zero of order mmm at s0s_0s0​, angles are multiplied by mmm.

    The argument principle

    We need this in the Nyquist unit, and it is a direct consequence of mapping, so it goes here.

    Let FFF be analytic in a region with finitely many poles and zeros. Draw a simple closed curve Γ\GammaΓ through no pole or zero, traversed counter-clockwise. Its image is a closed curve in the www-plane.

    If Γ\GammaΓ encloses ZZZ zeros and PPP poles (counted with order), then

    N=number of counter-clockwise turns of the image around the origin=Z−PN = \text{number of counter-clockwise turns of the image around the origin} = Z - PN=number of counter-clockwise turns of the image around the origin=Z−P

    Why, briefly. Near a zero of order mmm at z0z_0z0​, write F≈a(s−z0)mF \approx a(s - z_0)^mF≈a(s−z0​)m. The angle of FFF changes mmm times as fast as the angle of (s−z0)(s - z_0)(s−z0​). As sss goes once around z0z_0z0​, the angle of (s−z0)(s-z_0)(s−z0​) grows by 2π2\pi2π, so the angle of FFF grows by 2πm2\pi m2πm. A pole does the opposite and removes 2πm2\pi m2πm. A zero or pole outside Γ\GammaΓ gives no net change.

    Said another way, ∮ΓF′(s)F(s) ds=j ΔΓarg⁡F=2πj (Z−P)\oint_\Gamma \dfrac{F'(s)}{F(s)}\,ds = j\,\Delta_\Gamma \arg F = 2\pi j\,(Z - P)∮Γ​F(s)F′(s)​ds=jΔΓ​argF=2πj(Z−P).

    Summary

    • s=σ+jωs = \sigma + j\omegas=σ+jω packs a decay rate and a frequency into one number
    • An analytic function satisfies the Cauchy-Riemann conditions, and its complex derivative does not depend on direction
    • The map w=F(s)w = F(s)w=F(s) is conformal (angle-preserving) wherever F′≠0F' \ne 0F′=0

    • The frequency response is the image of the jωj\omegajω axis, and the argument principle is the base of the Nyquist criterion
