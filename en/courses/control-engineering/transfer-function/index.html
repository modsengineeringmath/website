    The Laplace Transform and Transfer Functions

    Build the mathematical model of a system from its differential equation, convert it to a transfer function G(s), find the poles and DC gain, and calculate the step response of an RC circuit and a mass-spring-damper system, with plots.

    Taught by Ajarn ModUpdated 12 Nov 2026About 35 min readฉบับภาษาไทย →

    What you can do after this unit

    • Write the differential equations of an RC circuit and a mass-spring-damper system
    • Convert a differential equation to a transfer function with the Laplace transform
    • Find the poles, zeros and DC gain of a transfer function
    • Calculate and sketch the step response of first-order systems and of second-order systems with real poles

    From a real system to an equation

    In Unit 1 we drew the plant as a box G(s)G(s)G(s) without asking what is inside. This unit builds G(s)G(s)G(s) from physical laws. The steps are the same for every system.

    1. Write the differential equation from the laws of the system, such as Kirchhoff's laws or Newton's second law.
    2. Take the Laplace transform of the whole equation with zero initial conditions.
    3. Rearrange as the ratio of output to input.

    The result of step 3 is the transfer function.

    Definition

    The transfer function of a linear time-invariant system is the ratio of the Laplace transform of the output to the Laplace transform of the input, with all initial conditions zero.

    G(s)=Y(s)U(s)G(s) = \frac{Y(s)}{U(s)}G(s)=U(s)Y(s)​

    Only one property of the Laplace transform is needed in this unit: differentiation in time becomes multiplication by sss when the initial conditions are zero.

    L{dydt}=sY(s),L{d2ydt2}=s2Y(s)\mathcal{L}\left\{\frac{dy}{dt}\right\} = sY(s), \qquad \mathcal{L}\left\{\frac{d^2y}{dt^2}\right\} = s^2Y(s)L{dtdy​}=sY(s),L{dt2d2y​}=s2Y(s)

    Example 2.2 Step response with real poles

    Let m=1m = 1m=1 kg, b=3b = 3b=3 N·s/m, k=2k = 2k=2 N/m. A constant force of 1 N is applied from t=0t = 0t=0. Find x(t)x(t)x(t).

    Step 1. Transfer function and poles.

    G(s)=1s2+3s+2=1(s+1)(s+2)G(s) = \frac{1}{s^2 + 3s + 2} = \frac{1}{(s + 1)(s + 2)}G(s)=s2+3s+21​=(s+1)(s+2)1​

    X(s)=1s(s+1)(s+2)=As+Bs+1+Cs+2X(s) = \frac{1}{s(s + 1)(s + 2)} = \frac{A}{s} + \frac{B}{s + 1} + \frac{C}{s + 2}X(s)=s(s+1)(s+2)1​=sA​+s+1B​+s+2C​

    Compare with the RC plot and note the difference at the start. A second-order system sets off with zero slope because the mass has inertia, while a first-order system sets off at its steepest slope.

    Poles give the shape of the response

    Each pole at s=−ps = -ps=−p contributes a term e−pte^{-pt}e−pt to the response. The pole nearer the imaginary axis (here s=−1s = -1s=−1) decays more slowly, so it decides how fast the system settles. It is called the dominant pole. If the poles are complex the response oscillates, which is the subject of Unit 6.
