    Exponential Functions: Graphs, Growth and Decay, Compound Interest, the Number e, Half-Life and Exponential Equations

    A linear function grows by adding the same amount again and again, while an exponential function grows by multiplying by the same factor again and again. At first the two can look close, but before long the multiplying one leaves the adding one far behind. This is the mathematics of compound interest, the spread of an infection, population growth and the discharge of a capacitor in an RC circuit. The page starts with the two shapes of the graph of bxb^xbx, explains where the number e comes from, and then uses it to compute interest and half-lives and to solve equations in which the unknown sits in the exponent.

    Definition and graphs of bˣ

    Each time x increases by one unit, bxb^xbx is multiplied by b, and that property is what separates exponential functions from polynomials. When b is greater than 1 the graph hugs the x-axis on the left and shoots up on the right; when b is between 0 and 1 the picture is reversed. Both kinds pass through (0, 1) because b0=1b^0 = 1b0=1, and neither ever touches the x-axis, since a power of a positive base is always positive. Note that (12)x=2−x\left(\tfrac12\right)^x = 2^{-x}(21​)x=2−x, so the graph for base ½ is the reflection of the graph for base 2 in the y-axis.

    Decay and half-life

    Exponential decay happens when the rate of decrease is proportional to the amount that remains: a radioactive isotope, a drug in the bloodstream, or the voltage of a capacitor discharging through a resistor. The half-life is the time needed to fall by half, and it is the same every time, whatever amount you start from. In electric circuits the time constant τ is used instead, and the half-life equals τln⁡2≈0.693τ\tau\ln 2 \approx 0.693\tauτln2≈0.693τ.

    Frequently asked questions

    Why must the base of an exponential function be positive and not 1?

    With a negative base, values such as (−2)^(1/2) are not real numbers, so the graph would break into scattered points. With base 1 you get 1^x = 1 for every x, a constant function with no inverse. The conditions b > 0 and b ≠ 1 make the graph continuous and one-to-one.

    Where does the number e come from?

    e ≈ 2.718 is the limit of (1 + 1/n)^n as n grows. You can read it as the value of 1 dollar at 100% annual interest compounded more and more often until it is continuous. e is also the only base for which the slope of the graph of e^x equals the value of the function, which is why it is the standard base in calculus and engineering.

    How much difference does monthly versus annual compounding make?

    Not much at low rates, but it adds up over time. 10,000 at 5% a year for 10 years grows to 16,288.95 with annual compounding, 16,470.09 with monthly compounding and 16,487.21 with continuous compounding. Compounding more often always helps, but it has a ceiling given by Pe^(rt).

    What is half-life and how is it calculated?

    Half-life is the time it takes for a quantity to fall to half, whatever amount you start with. The amount left is N = N₀(1/2)^(t/T), where T is the half-life. Carbon-14 has a half-life of about 5,730 years, so after 10,000 years about 29.8% of the original remains.
