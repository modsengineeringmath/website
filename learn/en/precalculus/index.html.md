// Chapter 27 · Precalculus · 16 pages


Precalculus is the stage where you stop treating an equation as something to solve and start seeing it as a function with a graph. Rush through it and calculus becomes hard for the wrong reasons: the limits and derivatives are fine, but the factoring, the domain or the shape of a logarithm are not. This chapter gathers the whole of precalculus into 16 pages in the order they build on each other, with formulas you can cite by ID, worked examples and interactive labs where you drag a value and the graph responds at once, on a laptop or a phone.

In 30 seconds

- Every topic here is a family of functions with its own domain, range, intercepts, asymptotes and end behavior

- $y = a\,f\big(b(x - h)\big) + k$ is a parent graph shifted, stretched and reflected, and it works for every family from parabolas to sine waves

- Exponentials and logarithms are inverses: $b^y = x \iff y = \log_b x$, so their graphs mirror each other in $y = x$

- Trigonometric functions are read from the unit circle: the point on the circle is $(\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$

- A sequence is a function on the natural numbers, a series is its sum, and statistics describes data with distributions

## Core formulas used throughout

H1 Domain rules

$$\frac{p}{q}:\ q \ne 0 \qquad \sqrt[2n]{u}:\ u \ge 0 \qquad \log_b u:\ u > 0$$

Functions with none of these (polynomials, $b^x$, $\sin x$) have domain $\mathbb R$

H2 Transformations

$$y = a\,f\big(b(x - h)\big) + k:\quad (x, y) \mapsto \left(\frac{x}{b} + h,\ a y + k\right)$$

h and b act horizontally (on x) · a and k act vertically (on y)

H3 Quadratic formula

$$ax^2 + bx + c = 0 \ \Rightarrow\ x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$$

H4 Exp ⇄ log

$$b^{y} = x \iff y = \log_b x \qquad (b > 0,\ b \ne 1,\ x > 0)$$

## Frequently asked questions

What is precalculus?
Precalculus is the mathematics you need to be fluent in before calculus: algebra, functions and their graphs, polynomial, rational, exponential and logarithmic functions, trigonometry, sequences and series, and often an introduction to probability and statistics. It corresponds to Grades 11–12 in North America, AP Precalculus, the functions and trigonometry parts of IB Mathematics, and Mathematics (Additional) for upper-secondary students in Thailand.

Why does this course focus on functions and graphs?
Every question in calculus is a question about a function. A limit is the height a graph approaches, a derivative is the slope of a graph and an integral is the area under it. Students who can look at a formula and see its graph learn calculus much faster, so every page here has a lab where you drag a value and watch the graph change.

Which page should I start with?
Start with the algebra review, then functions and graph transformations, because every later page reuses the same idea: a function has a domain, a range and a graph that can be shifted, stretched and reflected. After that, follow the order of your class or the learning path on this page.

Are there trig and statistics tables?
Yes. The trig table lists sin, cos, tan, cot, sec and csc for every degree from 0° to 360° with radians and exact values for the special angles. The statistics page has the standard normal table in both the cumulative Φ(z) form and the area-from-0-to-z form, plus Student's t, chi-square and F tables. Every value was recomputed with SciPy and every table can be searched.
