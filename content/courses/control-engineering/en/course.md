## About this edition {#about}

This is the English edition of the Thai classroom <a href="/courses/control-engineering/" lang="th" hreflang="th">วิศวกรรมระบบควบคุม</a>. The Thai edition is the source; each English unit is a full translation with the same theory, numbers, worked examples and exercises. The "go deeper" links at the end of each unit lead to the Thai reference library, where the formulas and interactive labs can be followed from the mathematics even without reading Thai.

## The examples come from electrical engineering {#electrical-examples}

The instructor teaches in an electrical power engineering programme, so every unit takes its examples from equipment that electrical students already handle. A reader with no background in control starts from a familiar picture and then moves to the standard theory used in international textbooks.

| Area | Examples used | Units |
|---|---|---|
| DC and AC circuits | RC, RL and RLC circuits, filters, contactor coils | 2, 3, 5, 6, 11 |
| Op-amp circuits and electronics | Feedback in op-amps, lead and lag compensators, analog PID | 1, 3, 10, 13 |
| Electrical machines | DC motors, induction motors with inverters | 3, 4, 5, 13 |
| Power systems | AVR, the swing equation, droop frequency control | 6, 7, 8 |
| Power electronics | Buck converter and its LC filter | 6, 11, 12 |
| Magnetic contactors, PLCs and DCS | On-off control, PID blocks, scan time, cascade loops | 1, 12, 13, 15 |
| Sensors and transducers | Probe time constants, 4–20 mA signals, encoders | 3, 5, 7 |
| Microcontrollers and digital circuits | ADC, PWM, sampling, digital PID | 15 |
| Fuzzy logic, AI and machine learning | Fuzzy rules, system identification from data, loop performance indices | 16, 17 |

## How to use each unit {#how-to-use}

1. **Read the outcomes** at the top first, so you know what you should be able to do at the end.
2. **Work each example** with pencil and paper. Do not only read it.
3. **Try the exercises before opening the answers.** The answers are folded; open them when you have finished.
4. Press **Mark as learned**. The course page then shows your progress. This is stored in your own browser only and is not sent anywhere.

:::note For instructors
Every unit has a **Classroom mode** button that enlarges the text and hides the menus for projecting, and a **Print / save as PDF** button for handouts. You may use the material in class with a reference to the site name and the page link.
:::

## What you should know first {#prerequisites}

- Linear ordinary differential equations of first and second order
- The Laplace transform and its inverse by partial fractions
- Complex numbers and basic circuit analysis
